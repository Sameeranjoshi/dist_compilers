\section{Open Problems}
\label{sec:open}

Each problem below is stated with the evidence in the catalog that motivates it.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Unified ownership and layout IR.} Affine distributions, mapping arrays, logical regions and mesh shardings are expressed in different IRs, and the MLIR shard dialect is the nearest candidate for a shared one~\cite{mlir2025shard,vaidya2025distributed}.
  \item \textbf{Dynamic and irregular partitioning.} Partitioners driven by connectivity or load and reuse of inspector results are shown in CHAOS and Kali, but reuse is guarded by conservative tests~\cite{ponnusamy1995runtime,mehrotra1990kali}.
  \item \textbf{Hierarchical heterogeneous systems.} Node, device and core levels are handled by nested mappings in Sequoia and by inter-/intra-operator plans in Alpa, not by a general mechanism~\cite{fatahalian2006sequoia,zheng2022alpa}.
  \item \textbf{Topology and memory capacity.} The analytic models in the catalog use communication volume without latency or bandwidth terms, and capacity appears only as a constraint~\cite{kong2023automatic}.
  \item \textbf{Correctness and consistency.} Verification of generated communication is rarely addressed; the PartIR core is the exception, with a proof of its lowering reported by its authors~\cite{alabed2025partir}.
  \item \textbf{Overlap.} Halo exchanges are sometimes non-blocking, but all-reduces are often blocking~\cite{bisbas2024shared,rocki2020cerebras}.
  \item \textbf{Composability and debuggability.} The systems in the catalog compose through schedules or sharding annotations, but the catalog records no study of debugging distributed programs generated by them.
  \item \textbf{Wafer-scale and fine-grained local memory.} Placement with per-element memory and neighbour routing reopens the placement problem, and the wafer-scale stencil compilers handle only star-shaped stencils~\cite{stawinoga2026mlir,rocki2020cerebras}.
\end{itemize}
