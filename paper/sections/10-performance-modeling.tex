\section{Performance Modeling and Optimization Objectives}
\label{sec:performance}

Three decision procedures appear in the read set: a closed-form or directive decision, an analytic optimisation over a cost model, and a search over configurations scored by profiles or a simulator (Table~\ref{tab:decision}). The procedure determines what the cost model has to represent, and the objectives it optimises.

\subsection{Analytic optimisation in the 1990s}
\fact{Gupta and Banerjee weight distribution constraints by estimated execution-time penalties, and the authors state that the combined problem is NP-hard and that their estimates rest on compile-time assumptions about loop bounds and branch probabilities~\cite{gupta1992automatic}.} \fact{Palermo et al.\ describe their static pipeline cost model as conservative~\cite{palermo1996dynamic}.} \fact{Lee's layout choice is a dynamic program over consecutive loops~\cite{lee1995techniques}.} \todo{Check how each analytic model in this group represents the network, from the text of \cite{gupta1992automatic,lee1995techniques,palermo1996dynamic}; the cards record the assumptions about loop bounds but not yet the network terms.}

\subsection{Volume models in the 2020s}
\fact{Kong et al.\ minimise local work plus a weighted communication volume under per-node memory capacity; the cost model is volume-only, with no latency or bandwidth terms, and the paper reports no numeric speedups in its text~\cite{kong2023automatic}.} \fact{GSPMD has no cost model; its sharding is chosen by priority-based propagation~\cite{xu2021gspmd}.}

\subsection{Measured and simulated costs from 2019}
\fact{PipeDream's optimiser uses a single-GPU profile of per-layer compute time, activation size and parameter size, and minimises the time of the slowest stage with a dynamic program over a hierarchical bandwidth model~\cite{narayanan2019pipedream}.} \fact{FlexFlow's simulator profiles each operator once and re-simulates incrementally, and the authors state that it assumes operator times are predictable and independent of input data~\cite{jia2019flexflow}.} \fact{Alpa uses profiled stage costs with an integer program for intra-operator choices and dynamic programming for stages~\cite{zheng2022alpa}, and PartIR's automatic tactic uses an analytical simulator with Monte Carlo tree search~\cite{alabed2025partir}.}

\subsection{What the objective shift implies}
Finding~2 and Finding~3 in Section~\ref{sec:history} give the counts. Taken together, the analytic group models the network and the search group models the program's execution. \infer{An analytic model needs a network cost accurate enough to rank choices; a profile or simulator needs only the cost of each operator on the target, which can be measured or simulated without a network model~\cite{jia2019flexflow,narayanan2019pipedream}. This is the inference behind the shift, and it is testable: a system that uses a network model and a profile on the same workload, compared on the same machine, would separate the two explanations. We did not find a read paper that makes this comparison.}

\subsection{Coverage of the objectives}
Communication volume is the most common objective across the eras, and local memory fit and productivity grow in the recent eras (Table~\ref{tab:objectives}). Two notes apply. First, productivity is recorded as an objective in seven of ten Era~6 papers, but none of the read papers measures productivity with a user study; the objective is asserted, not evaluated. Second, the objectives are recorded from the papers' stated goals, not from their measured outcomes.
