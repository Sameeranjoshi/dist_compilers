\section{Runtime Analysis and Irregular Computation}
\label{sec:runtime}

The boundary between compiler and runtime is where the systems differ most sharply. the runtime pattern in Section~\ref{sec:synthesis} states the central regularity: in the read set, every system that resolves irregular ownership has a runtime stage. This section explains the forms that stage takes and the costs they carry.

\subsection{Three forms of run-time stage}
The inspector–executor form separates an inspection of the index arrays from the execution that uses its result. \fact{Saltz and colleagues' executor runs a table-driven schedule built by a preprocessor, and the executor overhead is measured at about 9--10\% for sparse matrix-vector multiplication on an iPSC/2~\cite{saltz1990runtime}.} \fact{Kali caches the results of its inspector across repeated executions~\cite{mehrotra1990kali}.} The reuse condition is the decisive design point. \fact{Ponnusamy et al.\ reuse inspector results with a test that is conservative and can add overhead in some cases~\cite{ponnusamy1995runtime}.}

The dynamic-scheduling form moves the decision out of the compiler. \fact{Legion moves data-movement decisions to a runtime that reasons about logical regions and privileges~\cite{bauer2012legion}.} \fact{Dathathri et al.'s dataflow runtime evaluates task functions per node~\cite{dathathri2014thesis}.} The run-time-resolution form is a fallback. \fact{Rogers and Pingali combine run-time resolution, which is correct for every reference, with compile-time resolution where ownership can be analysed~\cite{rogers1994compiling}.}

\subsection{What the stage costs}
\infer{The stage costs an inspector pass or a dynamic scheduler on every change of the index arrays. The 1990s systems amortised this over repeated executions of the same loop~\cite{saltz1990runtime,mehrotra1990kali}, and the sparse tensor systems do the same over repeated tensor operations~\cite{yadav2022spdistal}. The adaptive variants accept a schedule rebuild when the indirection changes~\cite{hwang1995chaos}. We did not find a read paper that reports the break-even point against a fully static alternative for the same program, so the trade-off is described but not measured.}

\subsection{Where runtime stages are not needed}
Three read papers have no run-time stage for their distributions: Gupta and Banerjee's constraint method~\cite{gupta1992automatic}, Lee's dynamic program~\cite{lee1995techniques}, and Palermo et al.'s phase search~\cite{palermo1996dynamic}. All three decide the distribution at compile time, but over a cost model rather than by directive or analysis (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}). The compile-time decision is therefore not the same thing as a closed-form one, and the run-time question is separate from the decision-procedure question.
