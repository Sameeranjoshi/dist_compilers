\section{Multi-GPU, ML, Spatial, and AI-Accelerator Compilers}
\label{sec:modern}

The recent systems share one design choice: the programmer writes an unpartitioned model and a mesh, and the compiler propagates a layout through the program. The choice is the source of the propagation value on the owner-rule axis (Section~\ref{sec:synthesis}), and the rest of this section examines what it changes and what it leaves to the programmer.

\subsection{Propagation of shardings}
\fact{Mesh-TensorFlow expresses a program as named tensor dimensions mapped onto a mesh of processors, so any dimension can be split and the collectives follow from the layouts~\cite{shazeer2018meshtf}.} \fact{GSPMD completes sharding annotations by priority-based propagation and partitions every operator, covering data, model, spatial and pipeline parallelism through one mechanism~\cite{xu2021gspmd}.} \fact{PartIR separates partitioning tactics from the model and composes them on an MLIR-based IR, and its propagation uses rewrite rules rather than cost heuristics~\cite{alabed2025partir}.} \fact{Its authors report 50--62\% TPUv3 utilisation on up to 2048 cores for GSPMD, without a cost model or a search~\cite{xu2021gspmd}.}

\infer{Propagation moves the decision from the statements to the values. That makes the compiler's job a constraint problem over the graph, which is heuristic when the constraints are not unique. GSPMD's priority scheme and PartIR's tactic order are two answers to the same ambiguity, and PartIR's authors note that propagation can be blocked by reshapes and non-divisible shapes~\cite{alabed2025partir}.}

\subsection{Search over parallelism plans}
\fact{FlexFlow searches per-operator configurations with a simulator~\cite{jia2019flexflow}, and Alpa searches intra-operator shardings with an integer program and inter-operator stages with dynamic programming~\cite{zheng2022alpa}.} \infer{These systems automate the choice that the propagation systems leave to the programmer, the mesh shape and the stage boundaries. Alpa's authors state that cross-stage communication is not modelled~\cite{zheng2022alpa}, which is the same gap that the analytic group of Section~\ref{sec:performance} had for the network.}

\subsection{Pipeline and training systems}
\fact{PipeDream partitions layers into stages from a profile and schedules them with weight stashing~\cite{narayanan2019pipedream}.} \fact{GPipe places consecutive layers in cells whose boundaries balance an estimated cost, streams micro-batches through the cells, and inserts unnamed communication primitives at the boundaries; it is a library with no compiler, and the text gives no partitioning algorithm~\cite{huang2019gpipe}.} \fact{Megatron-LM splits transformer weights by hand, column-wise then row-wise, so that one pair of all-reduces per layer suffices, and states that it needs no compiler~\cite{shoeybi2019megatron}.} \fact{ZeRO keeps data parallelism and partitions optimizer states, gradients and parameters across the data-parallel processes; the runtime library fixes the partition from the data-parallel degree, and the stated communication volume equals data parallelism's for the first two stages and is 1.5 times it for the third~\cite{rajbhandari2020zero}.} \fact{None of the three reference lists cites a 1990s distribution compiler~\cite{huang2019gpipe,shoeybi2019megatron,rajbhandari2020zero}.} \infer{The three are the hand-derived and library-level instances of the decompositions that the propagation and search systems above automate; only GPipe's partitioner takes a placement decision at all.}

\subsection{Tensor and sparse-tensor compilers}
\fact{DISTAL and SpDISTAL bring distribution formats and scheduling commands to dense and sparse tensor algebra, compiled to Legion~\cite{yadav2022distal,yadav2022spdistal}.} \fact{Kong et al.\ derive mappings for tensor DAGs with an SMT solver~\cite{kong2023automatic}.} \fact{Vaidya et al.\ express distributed sparse tensors as an MLIR dialect that lowers to MPI, with partitions fixed by the programmer~\cite{vaidya2025distributed}.} \infer{The sparse systems are the only read papers with a sparse-tensor decomposition, and each keeps the partition choice with the user or with a run-time inspector.}

\subsection{Spatial and wafer-scale targets}
\fact{Rocki et al.\ hand-map a BiCGStab solver onto the Cerebras engine with one mesh column per core and a hardware-routed AllReduce~\cite{rocki2020cerebras}.} \fact{Jacquelin et al.\ map a three-dimensional stencil with one column of the domain per processing element and the third dimension in local memory; the decomposition, the routing colours and the block size are fixed by the programmer in the Cerebras language, and the preprint describes no compiler decision~\cite{jacquelin2022wse}.} \fact{Stawinoga et al.\ compile stencil DSLs to the Cerebras software language through three new dialects, with a runtime library that supports only star-shaped stencils~\cite{stawinoga2026mlir}.} \infer{All three read wafer-scale papers keep the decomposition static and place one tile per processing element. The compiler's contribution is the communication schedule and the mapping of loops to the fabric, not the choice of decomposition.}

The accelerator-vendor compilers named in the brief (Graphcore, SambaNova, Tenstorrent, Groq) are not catalogued, and no claim is made about them. The Shardy and MLIR shard material is official documentation rather than a paper. Both are carded from the documentation pages, so the claims that rest on them are documentation claims: the pages name no hardware, report no evaluation and carry no reference list~\cite{openxla2024shardy,mlir2025shard}.
