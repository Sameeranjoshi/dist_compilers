\section{Communication Generation and Redistribution}
\label{sec:communication}

Communication is derived in two ways in the catalog. Element-level generation computes, for each reference, which elements move between which places, from the subscripts and the distribution. Collective generation recognises an operator's pattern and emits a collective with participants fixed by the sharding. Which family a system records depends on the coding: the two codings disagree on most systems of Era~4 and Era~5, so we state no trend in the mix (Section~\ref{sec:synthesis}).

\subsection{Element-level generation}
\fact{Koelbel derived send, receive and iteration sets in closed form for forall loops over block and cyclic arrays with linear subscripts~\cite{koelbel1991regular}.} \fact{Chatterjee et al.\ generate local addresses and communication sets for cyclic($k$) distributions~\cite{chatterjee1993generating}.} \fact{Shen et al.\ remove iteration variables that do not change which element or processor is involved, reducing generated communication on one LU kernel~\cite{shen2007eliminating}.} \fact{Bondhugula's generator computes flow-out and write-out sets per tile and uses run-time helper functions to find the receiving processes~\cite{bondhugula2011automatic,bondhugula2013compiling}.} \infer{All four derive communication from affine index expressions. Their generality is limited by the affine assumption, and their cost is paid at compile time or in a small run-time helper. This is the classical regime of the catalog.}

\subsection{Collective generation}
\fact{GSPMD inserts collectives where propagated shardings disagree~\cite{xu2021gspmd}, and the MLIR shard dialect exposes all-gather, all-reduce, all-to-all, reduce-scatter and halo update as operations whose participants follow from the sharding~\cite{mlir2025shard}.} \fact{Kong et al.\ forbid the crossed mappings that would need point-to-point traffic, so only collectives are generated, and each (operator, tensor) communication vector selects a sub-communicator~\cite{kong2023automatic}.} \fact{Vaidya et al.\ implement blocking get, set and update-slice operations over MPI all-to-all and all-to-allv~\cite{vaidya2025distributed}.} \infer{Collective generation trades generality for a simpler model. Once the sharding is fixed the participants are known, so the cost of a collective can be stated in volume alone. Kong et al.'s model is volume-only for this reason, and it is the simplest model in the read set~\cite{kong2023automatic}.}

\subsection{Halo exchange and overlap}
Halo exchange is recorded in Eras~2, 3 and~6 under both codings. In Eras~4 and~5 it is recorded only under the first coding, so we do not claim that it recurs in every era (Section~\ref{sec:mapping}). Bisbas et al.\ lower declared halo exchanges to non-blocking sends and receives, and note that each halo is a separate exchange with no overlap and no diagonal exchange~\cite{bisbas2024shared}. Stawinoga et al.\ convert halo exchanges into chunked prefetches overlapped with computation on the wafer-scale engine~\cite{stawinoga2026mlir}. Rocki et al.\ overlap receiving with multiplication but keep the all-reduce blocking~\cite{rocki2020cerebras}.
\infer{Overlap is treated as a property of the halo or the chunk, and reductions are the usual exception. Among the read papers we did not find a reduction overlapped with the computation that feeds it; this is a candidate open problem, to be confirmed by a targeted search.}

\subsection{Redistribution}
Redistribution is the change of distribution between phases. Ramaswamy et al.\ represent regular distributions as families of line segments, so the data to move between two processor sets is their intersection~\cite{ramaswamy1996redistribution}. Palermo et al.\ choose phases by a shortest-path search that includes the redistribution cost~\cite{palermo1996dynamic}. In the recent systems redistribution is a resharding between two shardings, inserted where the propagation finds a mismatch~\cite{xu2021gspmd,alabed2025partir}. The concept is the same, the input is different: a pair of line-segment families in the 1990s, a pair of mesh shardings now.
