\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Compilers for distributed memory have solved the same three problems in every era: decide who owns each element, decide who computes each operation, and derive the communication between them. What has changed is the decision procedure and the cost the compiler tries to reduce. In the read set, a run-time stage accompanies data-dependent ownership; the cost models moved from analytic networks to measured and simulated programs; the optimisation objective moved from messages to memory and productivity; and the programmer's unit of reasoning moved from the statement to the operator. We state these as generalizations with their evidence strength and their refuting observations (Table~\ref{tab:generalizations}), and we state the open problems they leave.
