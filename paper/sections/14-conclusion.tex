\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Compilers for distributed memory have answered the same three questions in every era: who owns each element, who computes each operation, and what communication connects them. In the read set, the answers that survive two independent codings are narrow. Where ownership is set by an explicit map, the systems that describe a run-time mechanism for it have a run-time stage. Decompositions chosen by profile or search appear only from 2019. Message count led the objectives of the 1990s and is nearly absent later. Block-cyclic and mapping-array decompositions do not appear after 2010. Partition propagation appears only in the 2020s, beside owner-computes. We state these as generalizations with their evidence and their refuting observations (Table~\ref{tab:generalizations}). Several patterns that the first coding suggested did not survive the second, and we report them as withdrawn.
