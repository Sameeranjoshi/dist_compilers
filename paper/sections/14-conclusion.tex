\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Compilers for distributed memory have answered the same three questions in every era: who owns each element, who computes each operation, and what communication connects them. In the read set, the answers that survive two independent codings are narrow. Where ownership comes from an irregular or user-defined map and the system generates its communication, the read systems record a run-time stage in 17 of 22 cases under both codings, and the exceptions depend on the coding. Search-based decomposition is absent before 2019 under the intersection of the two codings, and each coding has earlier cases. Message count falls from the 1990s to Era~4 and is nearly absent later. Block-cyclic and mapping-array decompositions do not appear in read papers from 2010. Partition propagation appears only in the 2020s, beside owner-computes. We state these as generalizations with their evidence and their refuting observations (Table~\ref{tab:generalizations}). Several patterns that the first coding suggested did not survive the second, and we report them as withdrawn. The taxonomy, the catalog, the comparison tables and the sensitivity analysis are generated from the same records, so each count can be regenerated and challenged.
